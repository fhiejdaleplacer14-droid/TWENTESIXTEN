# TWENTESIXTEN — Story Short (Draft Script)

Format reference: the Roblox "storytime" short the developer shared (vertical 9:16, ~2:25,
first-person voiceover, a cut every 2–3 s, big word-by-word captions, floating pop-ups,
a twist at the end). Only the **format** is borrowed. The story is TWENTESIXTEN's own:
the camera → letter → memory → Lola → dream reveal chain that is already in the game.

- **Working title / caption:** "I went back to 2016 to see my Lola one last time"
- **Length:** ~2:20 (about 330 words of narration)
- **Frame:** 1080×1920, vertical
- **Voiceover:** English narration, so it reads to any audience. In-scene dialogue stays in Filipino with subtitles, using the lines already in `src/data/dialogues.js`.
- **Footage:** gameplay captures from the game itself (the pixel-art barangay) + a few simple "real life 2026" bedroom shots.

---

## Story in one paragraph

It's 2026. The narrator is homesick for one summer: 2016, the last one with Lola. Their phone's
"On This Day" memory shows a blurry photo of the old street. They fall asleep looking at it and
wake up *on that street*, young again. Everything is how it was: the sari-sari store, the
neighbor, Jun-jun missing shots at the court. But nobody recognizes them. Under the old bench they find
Lola's camera with a letter in the strap. The letter unlocks a flood of memories, so they run to the
end of the street, and Lola is there. She tells them she's here only because they never forgot her,
and that they have to go back. The barangay fades into light. They wake up in 2026. The twist:
they look at the blurry photo again and, for the first time, notice Lola in it, standing at the end of
the street, waving. It was only a dream. But for one afternoon, it was 2016 again.

## Style notes (copied from the reference format)

- **Captions:** one or two words at a time, centered at about 60% height, chunky rounded font, yellow fill with a thick black outline.
- **Pop-ups:** small HUD-style pills (`+ OLD CAMERA`, `MEMORY UNLOCKED`, `2016`). They play the same role the "+$500" money pop-ups play in the reference.
- **Pacing:** a cut every 2–3 s in sections 1–3. Slow down in section 4 (memory) and section 6 (fade), so the ending lands.
- **Music:** light, nostalgic acoustic/lo-fi for the 2016 parts. Drop it out completely before "And she was there." Use a soft piano for the ending.
- **Faces:** the reference relies on expressive face close-ups. For our pixel sprites, use zoomed-in portraits (or dialogue-box portraits) for the emotional beats.
